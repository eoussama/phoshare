The tour

Everything a social network does, minus the people

The "friends" below, lonely_lens, me.again and still_me, are all the same person. That's exactly the point.

01

Getting in

Accounts live on the device. Sign up with a username, a password and a security question, and you stay logged in until you log out. Forgot your password? Answer your question (case doesn't matter) and pick a new one.

02

The feed

Every post on the device, newest first, with favorites, comment counts and a share button. A brand-new device starts out a little lonely, and the app suggests the obvious fix: make another account.

03

Post, comment, share

Pick a photo from the system photo picker or take one with the camera app; neither needs a permission. Photos are turned upright and scaled down before they're saved. Open a post to read and write comments, or send the photo to another app through Android's share sheet.

04

Favorites and profiles

Tap the heart to keep a post in your favorites. Every account has a profile with an avatar, a bio, its join date and a grid of its photos. Your own profile is where you edit them, change your password, switch accounts or delete everything you posted.

05

Light and dark

Material 3 in the logo's own colors: warm yellow, slate gray and a touch of the rainbow stripe. It follows the system's dark mode, edge to edge, from Android 7 to Android 17.

Private by design

Nothing leaves the phone unless you send it

No internet permission

There is no server and no cloud. The app can't reach the network even if it wanted to.

No permissions at all

The system photo picker and the camera app hand photos over without the app asking for anything.

Hashed secrets

Passwords and security answers are stored as PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 hashes, 310,000 iterations with a random salt.

One way out

The only way a photo leaves the phone is when you share it to another app through the share sheet.

Build it yourself

Podman and Git. That's the whole setup.

No Android Studio, no JDK and no SDK. Java, the Android SDK, Gradle and adb all run in a container. One command builds the app, boots an Android 17 emulator and opens it in a window.

Read the commands 
# macOS and Linux
$ git clone https://github.com/eoussama/phoshare
$ cd phoshare
$ ./android.sh emulator --window

# Windows
> ./android.ps1 emulator --window

# or straight to your phone, over Wi-Fi
$ ./android.sh run 192.168.1.20:37000
